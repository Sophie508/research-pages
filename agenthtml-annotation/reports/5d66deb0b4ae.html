Definition

Intraday volatility, made explicit

Raw high–low spreads in index points punish large-level indexes. Voltasia standardizes to daily percentage range.

Primary metric

Average daily open range

intraday_vol = (High − Low) / Open × 100

Capital series is filtered to Date ≥ 2020-01-01, positive opens, and non-null highs/lows. Rows are averaged per Asia index ticker.

FAQ

Answers about the answer

Accordion details for methodology caveats seen in the trajectory.

Why not rank by absolute High − Low?

Index levels differ drastically (Nikkei vs Shenzhen points). Percentage of Open levels the comparison so “highest volatility” is economically comparable.

Why only six Asia indexes?

Metadata lists more exchanges (including Korea), but the trade table only carried continuous Asia bars for HSI, 000001.SS, N225, 399001.SZ, NSEI, and TWII.

Were messy dates a problem?

Yes — values arrive as ISO timestamps, “27 Jan 1987, 00:00”, and “October 07, 1987 at 12:00 AM”. The query tries three strptime patterns; final audit reported 0 unparsed dates.

How reproducible is the result?

The ranking is fully determined by the published SQL: join the Asia VALUES list, filter dates ≥ 2020-01-01, average 100×(High−Low)/Open, order descending. Top row is Shenzhen Component.

What was the original question?

“Which stock index in the Asia region has exhibited the highest average intraday volatility since 2020?” — answered by runnable Python/DuckDB analysis on the two provided databases.
