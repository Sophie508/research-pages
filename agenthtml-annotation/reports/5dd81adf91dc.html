Why it failed

  1. No purchase was completed. WestJet's payment gateway rejected the fabricated card, so no ticket was issued and no order confirmation exists. The agent's closing “✅ Task Completed” message overstates the result; its own earlier text correctly said the run stopped at the payment step.
  2. The eval signal never fired. With no successful POST, the interceptor had nothing to match, and the session was terminated on a 900-second idle timeout rather than on task completion.
  3. Cheapest was not strictly selected. $267 OTAs were seen and skipped in favour of the $295 direct fare.
  4. User data was invented. Five of eight identity fields diverge from the dossier, including the disposable email the benchmark watches.

Recurring mechanics worth noting

  • Ref churn. Google Flights' snapshots invalidated refs constantly; one type call failed for a missing ref and two clicks hit “element not found”.
  • Unsupported scroll act kind. The tool rejected kind:"scroll" twice (once at 07:16:52, once at 07:22:06); the agent worked around it by clicking the target directly.
  • Background tabs. Reseller “Continue to book” buttons opened new tabs silently; the agent only discovered this via browser tabs ~40 s later, and by then had pivoted to WestJet direct.
  • Arrow-key dropdowns. Months/provinces were chosen by blind ArrowDown counts — efficient-looking, but it produced the Nunavut mistake and a failed multi-key press.
  • One shell command (exec ls) was used to list /my-info/; the rest of the run stayed inside the browser, matching the instruction.
