Aircraft carrier USS Harry S. Truman (CVN-75) pulled into Souda Bay, Greece on Thursday for a port call following 50 days in the Red Sea, USNI News has learned.
The carrier transited the Suez Canal on Monday, a Navy official confirmed to USNI News.
Destroyer USS Jason Dunham (DDG-109) accompanied the carrier to Souda Bay, according to a social media post from U.S. Fleet Forces. Carrier Air Wing 1 is embarked on the Truman Carrier Strike Group.
Destroyer USS The Sullivans (DDG-68) is in the Mediterranean Sea but did not make a port call, a Navy official told USNI News. Destroyer USS Stout (DDG-55) and cruiser USS Gettysburg (CG-64) remain in the Red Sea.
This marks the third time in more than 15 months that the U.S. has left the Red Sea without an aircraft carrier. The last time was in November when the Abraham Lincoln Carrier Strike Group left the Middle East. Four destroyers maintained the U.S. presence in the Red Sea until the Harry S. Truman Carrier Strike Group arrived.
The U.S. has largely had a carrier strike group in the Middle East since November 2023, following the Oct. 7 Hamas attacks and subsequent Israeli bombardment of Gaza. The Houthis began their campaign against Red Sea shipping following the conflict between Israel and Hamas.
However, since Israel and Hamas struck a ceasefire, the Houthis stopped their attacks on merchant and Navy ships, although the Yemen-based group has continued its disinformation war against the U.S., most recently opening up a museum exhibit that included a diorama of an aircraft carrier explosion.
The Houthis said they are monitoring the ceasefire and will resume the attacks on ships if Israel violates it.
The Dwight D. Eisenhower Carrier Strike Group also made a port call in Souda Bay in May and June 2024, the only two port calls the ships made due to the high tensions in the Red Sea and Gulf of Aden.
