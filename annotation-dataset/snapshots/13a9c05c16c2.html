Original page: https://metro.co.uk/2017/03/16/moving-picture-shows-what-its-like-to-endure-six-years-of-war-6512465/
Here is a photo of a 70-year-old man, wearing socks and sandles and smoking a pipe, listening to music on a dusty phonograph while his bedroom lies all but destroyed around him.
People all over the world have been stirred by this image of an old man listening to his music, which perfectly captures the tragedy of the Syrian civil war – which has now raged for exactly six years.
So who is the man behind this iconic photo, which Instagram user Colin Harrison described as ‘a moment of tranquility amongst Hell on earth’?
The man on the bed is called Mohammed Mohiedin Anis – also known as ‘Abu Omar’ in his home town of Aleppo.
AFP photographer Joseph Eid spoke to Anis last year about his impressive collection of vintage American cars, which he was struggling to protect amid the worsening conflict.
‘He was a wealthy man,’ Eid told the Washington Post. ‘He speaks five languages. He studied medicine, went to Italy and had a lipstick enterprise.’
When the two first met, Anis showed off his car collection – with which he was obsessed.
‘When one of my cars is shelled, it’s like I or one of my relatives has been hit,’ he told Eid.
But it wasn’t easy to keep the cars safe. Neighbours even had to rally around to convince rebels in Aleppo not to mount a Dushka antiaircraft gun on top of his 1958 Chevrolet, according to AFP.
Now, with Aleppo back under government control, Eid revisited Anis last week – and found him living in destitution.
Anis’s two wives and eight children fled the city, but he stayed in the home where his ancestors were buried – even if it meant putting up with sporadic bombings.
And his beloved car collection is quickly dwindling – only a few remain. A collapsing wall had completely crushed his 1955 Buick Super.
‘Amid the rubble, we saw his cars destroyed and his house partially collapsed,’ Eid told the Post.
But even with all of this, Anis refused to leave.
‘Nothing will stop me from rebuilding, from living again,’ he told the AFP reporters. ‘It’s here I was born, and here I will die.’
It was at this moment that Eid realised, ‘it’s not a matter about cars anymore. It’s about an old man and his will to live’.
Anis showed the AFP reporters around his now-destroyed home, taking them into the bedroom and carefully sitting down on the bed.
He told them he liked classical music, gesturing towards the phonograph. Sceptically, one of the AFP team asked if it still worked.
‘Yes, of course. I will show you,’ Anis told them. ‘Wait! I cannot listen to music before lighting my pipe.’
The pipe, like everything else, was broken – Anis had taped it together.
As promised, he lit up the pipe, cranked a handle, and music filled the room.
Eid said: ‘This man is sitting in here, in his bedroom where he’s still sleeping, without windows, without a door. The wall is going to collapse I don’t know when.
‘And he’s listening to music. He’s smoking his pipe.’
