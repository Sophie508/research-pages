Key Takeaways
- To make a saltwater rinse, mix 1 teaspoon of salt into 8 ounces of warm water until fully dissolved.
- Swish the saltwater rinse in your mouth for 15 to 30 seconds up to four times a day and spit it out.
- Saltwater rinses are most helpful after dental procedures, for soothing sore throats, easing gum irritation, and supporting healing from minor mouth issues.
A saltwater rinse is a simple and effective way to support oral health by soothing gums and reducing bacteria. Swish with this easy solution up to four times daily to relieve discomfort and promote healing.
Making Saltwater Rinse at Home
To prepare a saltwater rinse:1
- Fill an 8-ounce glass with warm water.
- Add 1 teaspoon of salt and stir until it fully dissolves.
- Swish the solution in your mouth for 15 to 30 seconds, focusing on your teeth and gums.
- Spit it out.
- Use this rinse up to four times daily, or as your dentist advises.
Always prepare a fresh batch for each use. For extra benefits, add 1 teaspoon of baking soda to help freshen breath and prevent cavities.2
Saltwater Rinse Precautions
Saltwater rinses are usually safe and effective, but consider these precautions:
- Adjust the strength. If it feels too strong, reduce the salt to ½ teaspoon to be gentler on your mouth.
- Avoid swallowing. Always spit out the rinse to avoid increasing your sodium intake.
- Maintain oral hygiene. Keep brushing and flossing, as this rinse isn’t a substitute for them.
- Rinse before brushing. Using it afterward can wash away toothpaste’s protective fluoride.3
- Watch the frequency. Overuse may irritate your gums or soften enamel. Consult your dentist if unsure.4
If you have any medical conditions or concerns, talk to your dentist or healthcare provider before using a saltwater rinse. They can advise if it’s safe and right for you.
Benefits Over Mouthwash
Traditional mouthwashes often have alcohol in them. They can irritate swollen mucous membranes in the mouth. That’s a significant reason why dentists suggest salt water to relieve pain and swelling after a deep cleaning or other dental procedure.
Saltwater rinses have been proven to:
- Soothe oral ulcers or mouth sores5
- Reduce dental plaque and microbial count6
- Reduce bad breath
Dentists commonly recommend the use of a saltwater rinse after having wisdom teeth removed or other oral surgery.
Best Use for Saltwater Rinses
Gargling with a saltwater solution is a simple and effective way to maintain a healthy mouth. This rinse is commonly used for the following:
Healthy gums: Saltwater rinses can help by reducing bacteria that cause gum disease and tooth decay. They also soothe bleeding gums and support healing. One study found that rinses with 0.9% to 1.8% salt promoted gum health and recovery.4
Toothache: Rinsing with warm salt water two to three times a day may help to relieve a toothache because salt water works as an antiseptic. It gently removes bacteria from the infected area and helps reduce inflammation.7
Oral ulcers: Research shows that rinsing the mouth with a saltwater solution may help get rid of canker sores (aphthous ulcers). Not only does it help disinfect and soothe the ulcer, but it may also promote wound healing.4
After oral surgery: Your dentist may recommend a saltwater rinse after oral surgery, like a tooth extraction or gum procedure. It can help reduce inflammation and support healing. Research shows saltwater rinses aid recovery by keeping the area clean and promoting tissue repair.8
Sore throat: Gargling with salt water can help ease a sore or irritated throat. A 2019 study published in the Journal of Complementary and Alternative Medical Research found that gargling with a salt solution helped reduce pain and other symptoms associated with an inflamed throat (pharyngitis).9
Respiratory infections: Some research suggests that saline rinses, including gargling, may help shorten the duration of colds and other respiratory infections. In one small study, 93% of participants said it eased their symptoms and shortened their illness by about two days. A follow-up analysis found similar results for mild COVID-19 symptoms, though more research is needed.1011
Oral Baking Soda Paste for Canker Sores
To target a specific sore in your mouth, such as a canker sore, it’s easy to create a paste. Mix baking soda and small drops of water gradually until the paste is about as thick as toothpaste. Cover the canker sore with the paste. Repeat as often as necessary.
