Satoshi Nakamoto remains one of the most intriguing and mysterious figures in the cryptocurrency world. This fictitious creator launched Bitcoin in 2008 and contributed to its development until 2010, laying the foundation for the modern crypto ecosystem.1 Despite extensive speculation and investigation, Satoshi’s true identity remains unknown, fueling ongoing fascination and debate within the crypto community. Four prominent individuals have been suspected or accused of being Satoshi Nakamoto, though none have been definitively proven to hold that title.23
Key Takeaways
- Satoshi Nakamoto's true identity remains one of the most significant mysteries in the cryptocurrency world.
- Dorian Nakamoto was incorrectly identified as the creator of Bitcoin by Newsweek, impacting his privacy.
- Craig Wright claimed to be Bitcoin's founder, but a court found overwhelming evidence against his claims.
- Nick Szabo is considered a potential Bitcoin creator due to his work on Bit Gold and similar writings.
- It is widely believed that Satoshi Nakamoto could be a group of people rather than one individual.
Who Is Satoshi Nakamoto?
Nakamoto is important to the Bitcoin ecosystem beyond their status as a founder. The persona is a philosophical figurehead of sorts and is frequently invoked among cryptocurrency proponents debating the future of Bitcoin's development. For example, both sides in the contentious forking of Bitcoin Cash (itself a fork of the original Bitcoin blockchain) claimed to uphold Nakamoto's original vision for the currency.
It's difficult to truly know how much bitcoin Nakamoto owns, as it is suspected Nakamoto might have used different addresses when mining early blocks. However, some estimate Bitcoin wallets thought to be associated with Nakamoto hold several hundred BTC. However, most of these addresses are only theorized to belong to the legendary figure. Nakamoto's original address, used to send and receive the first bitcoins, holds more than 100 BTC and grows because it is continuously contributed to.4 Some believe Nakamoto has multiple addresses that total more than 1.1 million BTC.
Despite numerous efforts to uncover Nakamoto's elusive identity, only a handful of individuals have been proposed—none have been proven—to be Satoshi Nakamoto beyond a doubt. Here are four candidates.
Is Dorian Nakamoto the Real Satoshi Nakamoto?
One of the most high-profile attempts to reveal Bitcoin's founder occurred in March 2014, when Newsweek identified Dorian Nakamoto as the currency's creator.5
Newsweek claimed several similarities between Satoshi Nakamoto and Dorian Nakamoto. For example, both supposedly held libertarian leanings and a Japanese heritage. Dorian graduated in physics from California Polytechnic and worked on classified defense projects. The article's author also claimed Dorian Nakamoto said he was "no longer" involved with Bitcoin and that he had "turned it over" to other people.5
Fast Fact
The magazine’s biggest mistake was to publish a photograph of Nakamoto’s home. A cursory image search could easily reveal its location. While many did not believe Dorian Nakamoto was Bitcoin’s founder, the crypto community was aghast that his privacy had been violated.
Dorian Nakamoto later denied the quote, claiming that he had misunderstood the question and had nothing to do with Bitcoin.
Still, the media circus was not without profit for Dorian Nakamoto. In March 2014, tech entrepreneur and crypto-enthusiast Andreas Antonopoulos launched a fundraiser to support Nakamoto. The crowdfunding campaign served as an apology for the damage and difficulty Nakamoto experienced during the ordeal.6 More than 2,100 supporters donated 102.23 bitcoins worth roughly $34,500 in 2014; in Novemeber 2024, it would have been worth more than $7 million, but only 0.00370010 BTC (about $282) remained in the address.7
Craig Wright: Claims and Controversy Over Being Satoshi
For the most part, individuals suspected of being Satoshi Nakamoto have denied the claim or remained silent. However, that has not been the case with Craig Wright, an Australian with an interesting persona.
In December 2015, Wired Magazine wrote a profile on Wright, claiming it had "obtained the strongest evidence yet of Satoshi Nakamoto's true identity." The article reported on Wright's appearance via Skype at that year's Bitcoin Investor's Conference in Las Vegas. When asked about his credentials, Wright claimed he was "a bit of everything." He listed his degrees, including a master's in statistics and two doctorates. He also said: "I've been involved with all of this for a long time ... I try and keep my head down."8
Wired's evidence consisted of references to a "cryptocurrency paper" on Wright's blog that appeared months before the Bitcoin whitepaper began circulating. In addition, there were leaked emails and correspondence with Wright's lawyer referencing a "P2P distributed ledger." Furthermore, leaked transcripts of meetings with attorneys and tax officials quoted him as saying: "I did my best to try and hide the fact that I've been running Bitcoin since 2009. By the end of this, I think half the world is going to bloody know."8
Those claims were soon thrown into doubt. Wired followed up its report to note several inconsistencies in Wright's story. For example, the blog entries appeared to be backdated.9 Evidence also suggested that public encryption keys linked to Satoshi Nakamoto were also backdated.10 Even Ethereum co-founder Vitalik Buterin, who is otherwise reticent about politics in the cryptocurrency world, came out against Wright, publicly calling him a fraud.11
Wright, unfazed by the criticism, parlayed the media attention to carve out a prominent role within the crypto community. He led a contentious fork of Bitcoin Cash, forming Bitcoin SV.
In March 2024, London High Court Judge James Mellor found Wright not to be Satoshi Nakamoto. Mellor stated in his closing arguments that the evidence against Wright was overwhelming.12
The pronoun "we" is used throughout Nakamoto's Bitcoin white paper.13 Some suspect Nakamoto was a collection of individuals using a single anonymous identity.
Could Nick Szabo Be Behind the Satoshi Nakamoto Persona?
Nick Szabo is a computer engineer and legal scholar. He is credited with pioneering the concept of smart contracts in a 1996 paper.14 In 2008, he conceptualized a decentralized currency he called Bit Gold, a precursor to Bitcoin. He described Bit Gold as "a protocol whereby unforgeable costly bits could be created online with minimal dependence on trusted third parties."15 This is similar to the Bitcoin concept, whereby a network of computers without a leader verifies and validates transactions.
Author Dominic Frisby makes the case that Nick Szabo is Satoshi Nakamoto in his book Bitcoin: The Future of Money? Frisby consulted a stylometrics expert who concluded that Szabo's writing style was similar to known writings from Satoshi. Another clue is that both Szabo and Satoshi reference economist Carl Menger. In addition, Frisby learned Szabo had worked for DigiCash, an early attempt to bring cryptography to digital payments. In the author's eyes, this strongly suggested that Nick Szabo is Satoshi Nakamoto.16
Peter Todd's Possible Connection to Satoshi Nakamoto
In the most high-profile accusation yet, Peter Todd was accused of being Nakamoto by investigative filmmaker Cullen Hobak in a 2024 HBO documentary film, Money Electric. According to his GitHub page, Peter Todd is an "Applied Cryptography Consultant" who has been involved in Bitcoin development and other projects for many years.17
Remaining faithful to all known Bitcoin developer stances when asked about being the persona, Todd denied being Nakamoto. However, in his defense, the evidence presented in the documentary was circumstantial at best, and the Bitcoin developer community scoffed at the accusation.
It's doubtful anyone will uncover the identity or identities of Bitcoin's creators. The Bitcoin forum account that used the pseudonym was anonymous, and no evidence firmly points to anyone. Someone may know who it is, but so far, they have maintained their silence.
Who Satoshi Nakamoto?
If anyone knows who Satoshi Nakamoto is, they have remained silent. It is widely believed that Satoshi Nakamoto is a collection of people instead of one individual.
How Much Money Does Satoshi Nakamoto Have?
It is not publicly known how many bitcoins Nakamoto possesses, as it is suspected that Nakamoto used different addresses to mine blocks. However, it is collectively believed that Satoshi owns roughly 1.1 million bitcoins.
The Bottom Line
Satoshi Nakamoto’s significance extends beyond the mystery of their identity, having shaped Bitcoin and the crypto ecosystem. Several individuals, including Dorian Nakamoto, Craig Wright, Nick Szabo, and Peter Todd, have been suggested as possible candidates, though none have been confirmed. News organizations and crypto enthusiasts continue to investigate, yet Nakamoto’s identity remains elusive, perhaps on purpose, and may even represent a collective effort. Speculation also surrounds Nakamoto’s Bitcoin holdings, with estimates suggesting potential ownership of around 1.1 million tokens
