- Reports have emerged on X that popular streamer IShowSpeed was assaulted and robbed in Lagos, Nigeria during his African tour.
- There have been no legitimate reports of this occuring, and you can watch the pieces of the Lagos stream on Speed’s Twitch channel right now with no assault taking place.
- Fake images of the streamer with wounds on his face have been regurgitated by X’s AI.
Users on X are sharing images of popular streamer IShowSpeed after he was supposedly assaulted and robbed in Nigeria, to the point that the social media site is serving it as “news.”
The image in question portrays Speed as having wounds on his face, and is a edited screenshot from his most recent stream in Nigeria, which ended on Wednesday. The entire stream can be watched on his Twitch account right now, showing no such assault as the streamer enjoyed the night life of Lagos.
During the moment that the fake images portray, Speed is among crowds of fans who are exciting to meet him. He does not appear in any legitimate danger.
“IshowSpeed was allegedly attacked and robbed by road gangsters while livestreaming on Ikoyi Road in Lagos, Nigeria, resulting in a facial injury. He announced plans to leave Nigeria immediately and never return,” X’s AI-generated news report reads.
Another AI-generated report also says that there is no evidence that any assault happened to IShowSpeed while he was in Lagos.
The post comes from popular South African account Man’s Not Barry Roux, which has posted controversial statements in the past, including xenophobic posts about Nigerians living in South Africa. In a follow-up post, the account also suggested that “Nigerians are shameless” while alleging that IShowSpeed had to go into hiding in the country, which is likewise a false statement.
IShowSpeed shows a different side of Africa
The popular streamer, who recently hit 50 million subscribers on Twitch, is set to continue his long-running livestreamed tour of Africa.
So far his tour has drawn positive critical reception, with many commenters saying that Speed his portrayed a realistic Africa. An example is of his tour in Botswana, where it was revealed to Speed, and his legion of viewers that Botswanan locals are not legally allowed to purchase the diamonds that are mined in their own country due to corporate interests.
“I want to show the world what Africa really is,” said the streamer during his South African leg of the tour, when he raced a Cheetah and climbed ontop of a car while it was spinning.
“Bro, I’m not going to lie,” he told his chat during his first leg in Angola, “I love the love in Africa. The energy here is crazy.”
