Grosse Pointe cadaver dealer gets 9 years prison in body parts case
One woman wanted him to burn in hell. The government wanted him locked up for 14 years.
In the end, Grosse Pointe Park businessman Art Rathburn, a one-time prominent cadaver expert who built a $13-million empire cutting up bodies with chainsaws and renting their parts to medical researchers, got nine years in prison for fraud.
That's what U.S. District Judge Paul Borman concluded Rathburn, 63, deserved for scamming medical researchers out of $2.7 million by selling and renting them infected body parts, pretending they were disease free.
Rathburn's sentence was handed down on Tuesday, almost five years after the FBI raided his run-down Detroit warehouse and seized more than 1,000 body parts — heads, hands, legs, torsos — that were then stored in a deep freezer at the Wayne County Morgue as evidence of a crime.
Over time, the FBI managed to track down some of the relatives whose loved ones' remains wound up in Rathburn's possession. Among them was an Illinois woman, who donated her father's body to science eight years ago, not knowing it would be dismembered and sold for profit.
"Art Rathburn bought my father's head for $500," Tracy Smolka told the Free Press Tuesday, adding her father's torso was sold for another $2,900 as part of Rathburn's scheme.
Smolka, of Cook County, Ill., is among hundreds of people whom the government claims fell victim to Rathburn, who sold and rented infected body parts out of his run-down Detroit warehouse with the help of body donation centers in Arizona and Illinois.
"You can burn in hell," Smolka told Rathburn in U.S. District Court on Monday, calling him "disgusting, vile and reprehensible."
Rathburn was never charged with deceiving donors — only selling infected parts to unsuspecting researchers.

"We hope this sentence brings closure to the victims of Arthur Rathburn," U.S. Attorney Matthew Schneider said in a statement. "Rathburn's disgraceful conduct not only put the health of innocent people at risk, he showed a complete lack of regard for the donors and their families, who are all victims."
Rathburn showed no remorse during rambling remarks to the judge and blamed any problems that were encountered on the centers that supplied him with the bodies. He maintained the "bequests were put to great use" and denied using a chain saw to cut bodies.
"We have 10,000 diseases in this world. We know how to treat 500 of them. The rest need to be studied," Rathburn told Borman.
The defense maintained the case involved a contract dispute, not a crime.
In January, a federal jury convicted Rathburn of wire fraud, illegally transporting hazardous material and lying to federal investigators about his body parts scheme, which he ran out of a warehouse in Detroit through a business called International Biological Inc.
According to court documents, Rathburn purchased body parts from Arizona and Illinois suppliers, stored them in Detroit, then rented them out to medical and dental researchers with the help of his wife, Elizabeth Rathburn, who was also charged and convicted.
Elizabeth Rathburn pleaded guilty to wire fraud early on in the case, admitting that she knowingly took remains infected with HIV and hepatitis B to an anesthesiology conference in Washington, D.C., in 2012. She testified against her husband and got probation.
Rathburn, a former University of Michigan morgue attendant, was indicted in January 2016 following a years-long federal investigation dubbed "Body Brokers." The FBI had raided his warehouse in December 2013, seizing more than a thousand body parts.
Though Rathburn's name first surfaced a decade ago in a book called "Body Brokers," he did not fall under the FBI's radar until years later, when federal agents started tracking what appeared to be bizarre shipments arriving for Rathburn at Metro Airport, including a bucket full of human heads that had arrived from Israel.
Before getting into the body parts trade as a private dealer, Rathburn was the coordinator of the University of Michigan's anatomical donation program in 1984-90, but he was fired after he was caught selling bodies. In 1989, he started his own body broker business.
