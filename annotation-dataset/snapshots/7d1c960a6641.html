ABSTRACT
Aim of the study: This study seeks to evaluate the detrimental effects of acidic beverages on tooth structure
and overall dental health, with a particular focus on comparing the extent of damage at a microscopic level. While
it is widely understood that acidic drinks are harmful to teeth, the precise scale of the damage is often
underestimated. By examining the degree of harm caused by prolonged exposure to acidic substances, this
research aims to increase awareness of the cumulative effects that sugary and acidic beverages have on dental
health and aesthetics over time. Materials and methods: The teeth used in this study were obtained from private
practice, from patients diagnosed with Stage 3 or 4 periodontitis. These teeth were deemed unsuitable for
prosthetic treatment due to excessive clinical attachment loss (CAL) or advanced tooth mobility (grade 2 or 3).
Following extraction, the teeth were preserved in artificial saliva for a maximum of 20 days.The experiment
involved a sample of 30 teeth, each of which was submerged in a different beverage for a duration of 24 hours
The beverages used in this study were Sprite, lime juice, apple vinegar, and white wine. Following the 24-hour
exposure, the teeth were documented, and photographs were taken. The enamel surface of the teeth was then
analyzed under a Scanning Electron Microscope (SEM) to examine the extent of acid-induced damage in detail.
Additionally, the density of the two halves of each tooth was compared using a Diagnodent pen (KaVo) to further
assess the impact of the acidic beverages. Results: The results of this study will contribute to a deeper
understanding of enamel erosion caused by acidic beverages, offering valuable insights for both dental
professionals and individuals aiming to protect their enamel from deterioration. Conclusions: In conclusion, the
excessive and frequent consumption of acidic beverages results in irreversible enamel demineralization, leading
to aesthetic concerns such as white spots, discoloration, surface roughness, and an increased risk of tooth
sensitivity or loss. Additionally, the method of consumption—particularly the tendency to a prolonged the contact
between these beverages and the dental structure—exacerbates the potential for damage. To minimize these
effects, it is imperative to reduce both the frequency of acidic drink intake and the duration of their contact with
the teeth. Effective management of acidic beverage consumption is essential for preserving oral health and
maintaining dental aesthetics.
Key words: enamel, demineralization, acid, erosion, Diagnodent, SEM, aesthetics.
INTRODUCTION
Teeth are composed of several layers, with
the outermost being the enamel, which serves
as a critical protective barrier against external
factors such as acids, bacteria, and mechanical
stress that can damage the teeth. Enamel,
recognized as the hardest substance in the
human body, plays a vital role in maintaining
dental health. However, its durability does not
render it immune to damage. A key factor
negatively
impacting
dental
health,
particularly the enamel, is the consumption of
acidic beverages. (1)
47
Acidic drinks such as Red Bull, Coca-Cola,
orange juice, and similar beverages, contain
acids like citric or phosphoric acid. (3,4) These
acids lower the pH in the oral environment,
thereby creating an acidic condition that can
initiate
a
process
known
as
enamel
demineralization. (1)
The adherence of food and beverages to
dental surfaces plays a significant role in the
adhesion and proliferation of microorganisms.
Consequently, compounds with antimicrobial
properties are of substantial interest in dental
research. Phenolic compounds, which are
abundant in red wine and black coffee, have
garnered
attention
for
their
potential
antimicrobial effects. Given the widespread
consumption
of
these
beverages,
understanding their impact on dental substrate
is an important focus of investigation. (7)
Dental aesthetics plays an essential role not
only in enhancing the appearance of a smile
but also in preserving the structural integrity of
teeth. One important factor affecting dental
aesthetics is enamel health, which can be
significantly compromised by dietary habits,
particularly
the
acidic
beverages. (10) When the acidic compounds of
beverages
interact
with
hydroxyapatite crystals of the enamel, they
trigger a process known as demineralization,
leading to the dissolution of calcium and
phosphate
ions.
Over
time,
prolonged
exposure to such acids weakens the enamel,
making teeth more vulnerable to decay,
discoloration, and increased sensitivity. (9)
Several factors influence the extent of the
impact acidic drinks have on enamel. One of
the most significant is the duration of exposure
to the acids. Prolonged contact with acidic
beverages amplifies the potential for enamel
damage, as continuous sipping or holding the
liquid in the mouth extends the exposure time
of the acids to the enamel surface, leading to
greater demineralization and erosion. (3)
In addition, the frequency of consumption
and the concentration of acids in the beverages
play a critical role in the severity of enamel
erosion. Individuals who frequently consume
acidic drinks face an elevated risk of enamel
degradation. (4)
In the following in-vitro experiment, four
extracted teeth will be exposed to multiple
acidic beverages, including Sprite, lime juice,
vinegar, and white wine. Prior to and after
exposure, the teeth will be divided into two
equal halves and subjected to scanning with a
Diagnodent scanner. Additionally, they will be
examined
under
Scanning
Electron
Microscope (SEM) to capture detailed images
of the enamel surface before and after
exposure, offering insights into how the acids
in these drinks interact with and degrade the
enamel layer.
The primary objective of this study is to
investigate the effects of acidic drinks on the
enamel surface of teeth, aiming to understand
the contribution of such beverages to enamel
demineralization and the potential long-term
consequences of repeated exposure. The
experiment will evaluate key factors such as
exposure duration, frequency of consumption,
and acid concentration in the beverages to
determine their impact on enamel integrity.
The findings of this study will enhance our
understanding of enamel erosion caused by
acidic drinks and provide valuable insights for
both dental professionals and individuals
seeking to protect their enamel from damage.
Understanding the relationship between
enamel demineralization and dietary choices is
essential for promoting both oral health and
esthetic outcomes.
MATHERIALS AND METHODS
The teeth used in this study were obtained
from private practice, from patients diagnosed
48
with Stage 3 or 4 Periodontitis. These teeth
were
deemed
unsuitable
prosthetic
treatment due to excessive clinical attachment
loss (CAL) or advanced tooth mobility (grade
2 or 3). Following extraction, the teeth were
preserved in artificial saliva for a maximum of
20 days. Each tooth was carefully examined
for external damage caused by trauma or
caries, and only those without damage were
selected as suitable samples.
Once
the appropriate
samples
identified, they were bisected vertically along
the long axis using a turbine equipped with
diamond-coated
burs,
after
which
experiment was conducted.
All patients were fully informed about the
experiment, and their consent was obtained for
the use of their extracted teeth in this study.
The experiment involved a sample of 30
teeth, each of which was submerged in a
different beverage for a duration of 24 hours.
The teeth were bisected, with only one half of
each tooth being exposed to the beverages,
while the other half was reserved for later
comparison. The beverages used in this study
were Sprite, lime juice, apple vinegar, and
white wine.
Following the 24-hour exposure, the teeth
were documented, and photographs were
taken. The enamel surface of the teeth was
then analyzed under a Scanning Electron
Microscope (SEM) to examine the extent of
acid-induced damage in detail. Additionally,
the density of the two halves of each tooth was
compared using a Diagnodent pen (KaVo) to
further assess the impact of the acidic
beverages.
Diagnodent Analysis
The teeth will be examined using a
Diagnodent instrument to assess the degree of
demineralization both prior to and following
the treatment. The Diagnodent Pen from KaVo
serves as a device for the early detection of
caries, utilizing low-level, radiation-free laser
technology with a wavelength of 655 nm based
on fluorescence principles.This method is non-
invasive, providing a comfortable experience
for patients without inducing pain, and it
significantly
reduces
abrasion
compared to traditional manual probing
techniques.
SEM Analysis
A Scanning Electron Microscope (SEM)
employs a low-energy electron beam that
interacts with a material to scan the surface of
the sample. Upon contact with the surface,
various reactions occur, resulting in the
emission of photons and electrons from or near
the surface. The interaction between the
electron beam and the sample's atoms
generates
distinct
signals
that
provide
information regarding the surface topography
of the sample.In this study, we utilized a
Scanning Electron Microscope (SEM JEO) in
collaboration with the Faculty of Chemistry at
the University of West Timisoara. Scanning
images were captured and displayed on the
monitor
at
varying
magnifications.We
conducted an analysis for each solution
utilized, examining both the untreated and
treated halves to facilitate a comparative
assessment of the two conditions.

In a 2015 in vitro study (1), all tested soft
drinks, with the exception of yogurt, were
found to exhibit erosive properties. The
erosion of enamel caused by Coca-Cola,
orange juice, Cedevita, and Guarana was
found to increase with prolonged exposure
times.
Another study examining the surface
roughness of extracted teeth (2) indicated that
exposure to soft drinks resulted in an increase
in surface roughness. A 2022 study (3)
concluded that vinegar and apple cider are the
most harmful dietary preservatives, while
lemon juice was identified as the most
detrimental acidic beverage. Additionally,
Coca-Cola
and
Mountain
Dew
recognized as potent demineralizing agents.
To mitigate the effects of acidic beverages,
oral health educators can advocate for
practices such as reducing the duration and
frequency of beverage contact, as well as
employing fluoride applications, buffering
agents, and mouth rinses following the
consumption of acidic drinks.
A study conducted in 2022 reports that
Noncarious cervical lesions (NCCLs) present
as smooth, saucer-like depressions or V-
shaped notches on the teeth. The prevalence
of these lesions varies significantly, with
reports indicating values ranging from as low
as 10% to as high as 90%. Contributing
factors to their formation include habits such
as excessive tooth brushing and the
consumption of an acidic diet. (8)
Several other studies (4, 5, 6) consistently
concluded that longer exposure times to
acidic beverages correlate with increased
severity of enamel erosion.
Most studies (2, 3, 4) recommend limiting
the duration of exposure to acidic drinks.
Rather
than
sipping
throughout the day, it is advisable to consume
them within a shorter time frame and
subsequently rinse the mouth with water to
help neutralize the acids. Furthermore, using
a straw while drinking acidic beverages can
minimize direct contact with the teeth,
thereby reducing the exposure duration.
Maintaining adequate oral hygiene (2, 3, 5, 6)
is also a crucial factor in preserving the
enamel surface and protecting it from various
forms of exposure.

In our study we utilized a sample of 30
teeth, each submerged in a different beverage
for 24 hours. Each tooth was bisected, with
one half exposed to the selected beverage
while the other half was retained as a control
for subsequent comparison. The beverages
tested included Sprite, lime juice, vinegar,
and white wine. After the 24-hour exposure
period,
teeth
documented
photographically. The enamel surfaces were
then examined in detail using a Scanning
Electron Microscope (SEM) to assess the
extent of acid-induced damage. Furthermore,
the density of each tooth’s halves was
evaluated using a Diagnodent pen (KaVo) to
provide additional insights into the effects of
these acidic beverages. Excessive and
improper consumption of acidic beverages
poses a substantial risk to dental health,
primarily through enamel demineralization.
Over time, this can lead to visible aesthetic
issues such as discoloration, chalky white
spots, and surface roughness, eventually
progressing to caries and even tooth loss if
preventive measures are not taken. The
manner in which acidic drinks are consumed
can further exacerbate these effects by
increasing acid exposure to the enamel.
Limiting both the frequency and duration of
exposure to acidic beverages, along with
reducing overall consumption, is essential to
minimizing enamel damage. While moderate
consumption may not cause immediate harm,
consistent, frequent intake will inevitably
degrade enamel, impacting both the structural
integrity and aesthetic quality of teeth.
Conclusions
1. Excessive
inevitably
leads
to
demineralization of the enamel surface,
resulting in the formation of inesthetic
chalky white spots. If no preventive
measures are taken, this condition may
eventually progress to caries. The acids
present in these beverages affects the
tooth surface, and if consumption is
excessive, the teeth may become more
sensitive or even sustain permanent
damage.
2. The manner of consumption also plays a
significant role, although this was not
specifically addressed in the present
experiment. There is a notable difference
between simply drinking a beverage and
allowing it to linger or be "dragged"
through the teeth. Some individuals
engage
in
this
behavior,
thereby
increasing the contact time between the
acidic beverage and the tooth surface,
which exacerbates the risk of damage.
3. It is advisable to limit the frequency of
acidic beverage consumption throughout
the day and to minimize the duration that
the drink remains in the mouth.
Implementing such measures, along with
an overall reduction in the intake of these
drinks, will help lower the risk of enamel
demineralization.
4. In most cases, the demineralization
caused by acidic beverages cannot be
reversed, often leading to carious lesions,
and aesthetics alteration such as tooth
discoloration, loss of the natural shine,
surface roughness and, in the most severe
cases, eventual tooth loss.
5. The irreversible demineralization of
enamel caused by frequent consumption
not
only
compromises the structural integrity of
teeth but also significantly affects dental
aesthetics. This enamel degradation can
lead to discoloration, rough surface
textures, and an overall dull appearance,
diminishing the natural shine and
attractiveness of the teeth. Moreover, as
the enamel weakens and carious lesions
develop,
aesthetic
restorations
may
become necessary. Therefore, proper
management of acidic drink consumption
is crucial not only for preserving oral
53
health but also for maintaining a pleasing
dental appearance.
6. In
conclusion,
while
moderate
consumption of acidic beverages may not
immediately
result
demineralization, frequent and improper
consumption will undoubtedly cause
damage over time.
