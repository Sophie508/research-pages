DISCUSSION 
5.1 
Background 
5.1.1 
What has changed? 
Commencing in 2023, the City undertook a comprehensive rating structure review with the 
assistance of an expert consultant. The objectives were to:  
• 
ensure the rating structure is fair and equitable for all ratepayers 
• 
identify additional strategies and opportunities for general rates revenue growth 
• 
ensure the rating structure is contemporary and efficient to administer, and 
• 
ensure the rating structure meets the future needs of the City and is easy to 
understand.

As part of the 2024-25 budget process options and opportunities identified through the rating 
category review were presented to Council. Following comprehensive discussion, Council 
adopted, as part of the 2024-25 budget, a new rating category structure for the general rate 
on PPR Vertical Community Titles Scheme (CTS) lots.  
• 
The adopted rating structure included additional differential general rating categories 
for PPR vertical CTS banded based on the area of the CTS lot (Unit) and the level 
above ground on which the lot was located. The change in the rating category was to 
improve equity and fairness for all ratepayers across the City. 
• 
The change to the rating structure adopted in 2024-25 is about ensuring that all 
properties across the City make a meaningful and fair contribution through general 
rates that are consistent with the principles of valuation-based rating. Higher-valued 
properties pay more general rates than lower-valued properties used in the same 
way. 
• 
No 'extra’ revenue was raised from the change. The change resulted in a 
redistribution of revenue across the rate base.  
• 
If these changes had not been made to the general rating categories for PPR Vertical 
CTS the remaining 96% of properties across the City would have experienced higher 
general rate increases. 
• 
The Valuer General does not provide individual valuations for each lot within a CTS, 
instead, one valuation is issued for the CTS property that is ‘shared’ between the lots 
in the CTS. The adopted rating structure has been implemented to ensure lots in a 
CTS pay a meaningful and fair contribution through general rates using the ‘shared’ 
valuations. 
• 
Approximately 12,200 properties, or 4% of all ratepayers across the City, were 
affected by the change.  
• 
When considering the increases in dollar terms, the changes have resulted in an 
increase of between $3.20 per week for those on levels 5 to 10, up to a maximum of 
$13 per week for properties located above level 40 (97 properties across the City 
received the maximum increase of approximately $13 per week (less than 1% of 
affected properties and less than 0.03% of all rateable properties). 
• 
Council remains committed to keeping rates and charges increases as low as 
possible for PPR residential properties, including those in vertical CTS. In the 2024-
25 financial year, total rates and charges for a PPR single unit dwelling with a median 
rateable valuation increased by just 4.24%, which is less than the average inflation for 
the previous 12 months of 4.8%.  
• 
Whilst the general rate for the vertical PPR CTS in 2024-25 has increased, this is to 
achieve a more equitable general rate than was previously levied. 
• 
Approximately 51% of residential properties across the City are part of a CTS. The 
number of vertical CTS properties will also continue to grow in line with increased 
density.  
• 
The City landscape has changed, therefore if this was not corrected it would further 
exacerbate the inequity in the future. 
• 
The City’s rating structure needs to ensure appropriate and equitable general rates 
are levied on all ratepayers and to make sure that non-CTS properties are not levied 
a disproportionately high contribution towards General Rates.

5.1.2 
Why was the change made? 
Council levies general rates based on the value of the land in accordance with the Local 
Government Act 2009 and Local Government Regulation 2012.  
The land valuation is applied to the rate in the dollar for the relevant differential general rate 
category to determine the general rates payable for the property, subject to a minimum 
general rate also being applied.  
This valuation-based approach to general rates implies that higher-valued properties pay 
higher general rates compared to lower-valued properties that are used in the same way. 
General rates are the primary funding source for Council. They are levied to fund the 
infrastructure, services, facilities, and activities for the community in general and are not 
contingent on the use of services or infrastructure by an individual property. 
For all lots outside of a CTS, Council receives a single valuation for each lot. These 
valuations provide a consistent basis on which to levy general rates as each valuation is 
specific to the lot and is a fair representation of its value relative to those around it. 
However, for CTS properties, Council receives a single valuation from the Valuer General for 
the land on which the CTS is constructed. This single valuation is ‘shared’ between the 
individual lots in the CTS in accordance with the lot interest entitlement schedule contained 
in the Community Management Statement for the CTS. This ‘shared’ rateable valuation for a 
CTS lot is usually very low and is dependent on the number of individual lots in a CTS. The 
more lots that are contained within a CTS, the lower the ‘shared’ valuation that will apply to 
the individual lots. As a result, it does not provide a consistent and reliable measure of one 
lot’s relative value compared to another.  
When used to calculate general rates, the ‘shared’ CTS valuations do not, on their own, 
always provide an adequate mechanism to ensure higher-valued properties pay higher rates 
compared to lower-valued properties. 
In addition to improving equity in the general rates paid between CTS lots within the same or 
similar CTS properties, the adopted rating structure for 2024-25 also goes some way to 
bridging the gap in general rates paid by valuable CTS units relative to non-CTS lots with a 
comparable property value. 
To calculate general rates, the Valuer General provides Council with the site valuation of a 
property and not market valuation, however the two methods of valuation trend together, 
albeit with the site valuation generally much lower than the market valuation. Accordingly, it 
is reasonable to expect a CTS and Non-CTS lot with a comparable market valuation should 
be required to make a similar contribution through general rates. 
The rating structure adopted for PPR vertical CTS properties for 2024-25 goes some way to 
recognising the effect that the floor area and floor level have on the value of the unit and 
applies an increased minimum general rate in accordance with the principle of valuation-
based rating thus creating greater fairness and equity across all City ratepayers. Importantly, 
the site valuation continues to be the foundation on which general rates are based with the 
differential general rating categories based on the height and area attributes of the CTS lot.

5.1.3 
What do other Councils do? 
The difficulty that arises from the use of ‘shared’ rateable valuations for CTS lots is not 
unique to the City of Gold Coast. It is an issue for all Queensland local governments, but 
especially those that have a large proportion of properties that are part of community title 
schemes. At the City of Gold Coast, 51% of all residential properties are part of a CTS and 
this is expected to increase into the future. 
Other SEQ Councils that have implemented specific general rating structures to try to 
address this issue include: 
• 
Brisbane City – have developed a ‘parity factor’ that applies to large vertical CTS based 
on the site valuation assigned to the land on which the CTS complex is built. The 
calculation of the ‘parity factor’ is complex and highly bespoke to the BCC property base 
and their corresponding rateable valuations. 
• 
City of Moreton Bay – have adopted 12 differential general rating categories (6 for PPR 
and 6 for NPPR) that are banded based on a calculation of the valuation per square 
metre of the land on which the CTS is constructed. This approach assists to identify and 
differentiate higher valued CTS properties, however does not recognise more valuable 
units within a single CTS property. 
• 
Sunshine Coast – have adopted 6 differential general rating categories (2 each for PPR, 
NPPR and TR). For each property type there is one category applicable to Low-Rise 
units (no more than 4 levels), and one for High-Rise units (greater than 4 levels). This 
approach recognises high-rise CTS properties from low-rise; however does not recognise 
the differing value of lots within a CTS property. 
The ideal system of rating CTS lots would require the Valuer General to issue a separate 
valuation for each lot in a CTS rather than the current approach that implies a ‘shared’ 
valuation for each lot. In the absence of that, there is no single ideal method for ensuring 
equity and fairness in the calculation of general rates for CTS lots, as evidenced by the 
variety of different approaches employed by Councils in attempting to deal with the ‘shared’ 
valuation anomaly.

Is the change legal? 
The adopted 2024-25 rating structure for PPR vertical CTS is consistent with the approach 
that has been applied to NPPR/PR and TR vertical CTS since 2006 and complies with the 
Local Government Act 2009 and Local Government Regulation 2012. 
It is important to note that general rates are not a fee for service or user pays charge. 
General rates are levied on rateable properties in the City to fund the infrastructure, services, 
facilities, and activities for the community in general and are not contingent on the use of 
services or infrastructure by an individual property. General rates are calculated by using the 
site valuation for properties, implying that properties that have a higher valuation should pay 
higher general rates than lower-valued properties used in the same way. 
It is also important to note that the general rates are calculated based on the site valuation 
for the property and not the market value. Assertions that the 2024-25 adopted rating 
structure for PPR vertical CTS is using market value for the calculation are incorrect. The 
area of the CTS lot and the level on which the CTS lot is located are attributes of the property 
and are used in the categorisation of the CTS lot for general rates; however, the calculation 
of the general rates utilises the ‘shared’ site valuation.

Petition Theme 
1 
The Council should apply the same rate basis when rating / valuing 
property.   Homeowners are rated according to their land value 
whereas apartment owners are rated according to indicators of 
property value. 
Response 
Section 74 (1) of the Local Government Regulation 2012 (the Regulation) mandates that 
Council must calculate the rates for the land by using the rateable value of the land. It is 
not possible for Council to adopt a general rating methodology that does not use the same 
rateable valuation basis. 
The adopted rating structure for 2024-25 is still dependent on property values at the core 
of calculating general rates, however, is structured to assist in achieving greater equity 
when the valuation is too low through the application of an appropriate minimum general 
rate. 
 
Petition Theme 
2 
Council has increased the general rates we pay but we are not 
receiving any additional services. 
Response 
Council general rates are levied to fund the infrastructure, services, facilities, and activities 
for the community in general and are not contingent on the use of services or 
infrastructure by an individual property.    
General rates are calculated in accordance with the Local Government Act 2009 which 
implies that higher valued properties should pay higher general rates that lower valued 
properties used in the same way. 
The increase in general rates for PPR vertical CTS properties in 2024-25, is not implying 
they use more services or facilities than previously – but merely an increase to ensure an 
appropriate contribution is being made through general rates in accordance with the 
principles of valuation-based rating. 
 
Petition Theme 
3 
Rate increase places a further burden on pensioners and those living 
on fixed income who cannot afford it. 
Response 
The Council levies general rates on the property based on attributes of the property and 
how it is used. It is not possible to differentiate pensioner owned properties via differential 
general rates. 
Instead, Council provides support to pensioners through pensioner rates rebates to 
reduce the rates liability. In addition, ratepayers who are unable to pay their rates on time 
can contact Council to arrange to pay their rates off over time through generous interest 
free payment plans.

Petition Theme 
4 
The additional tax is imposed arbitrarily, is unjust and unfounded. 
Response 
It is important to note, the change implemented in 2024-25 is not an additional tax. It is a 
new structure for determining the general rates on PPR vertical CTS lots and replaces the 
previous method used by the City. 
The 2024-25 rating structure has been implemented to assist in addressing an inequity 
with the general rates levied on vertical CTS units that exists because of the way that 
rateable valuations for CTS lots are determined. The 2024-25 structure recognises factors 
that affect the value of a unit, namely the size (floor area) and floor level above ground 
and applies an increase to the rate and minimum general rate as these attributes 
increase. 
A fundamental principle of general rates is that higher value properties ought to pay more 
general rates than lower valued properties used in the same way. The method in which 
rateable valuations must be calculated for vertical CTS properties does not, on its own, 
provide a reliable and consistent method of ensuring the principle of valuation-based 
rating is adhered to.    
 
Petition Theme 
5 
Council should calculate general rates "Lot Entitlement" the same as 
water charges 
Response 
Community titles schemes are established under the Body Corporate and Community 
Management Act 1997. When a CTS is established a Community management Statement 
(CMS) must be prepared that all the essential information for the body corporate, including 
the schedule of lot entitlements. 
There are two forms of lot entitlements: 
1. Contribution entitlement – This is used to determine the proportion of body 
corporate levies each owner is liable for and is also used by the Council to apportion 
utility charges, e.g. water charges. 
2. Interest entitlement – determines each lot owners share of common property, their 
interest on termination of the body corporate, and must also be used by Council to 
apportion the site valuation provided for the calculation of general rates. 
It is not possible for Council to calculate general rates using the same contribution 
entitlement that is used to apportion water charges. Even if this was possible, it would still 
yield small and inconsistent rateable valuations for lots and would not resolve the 
underlying issue we are seeking to address with the new rating structure.

Attachment 3 – Community Feedback Summary and Response 
The community were given an opportunity to provide feedback on the changes through a 
campaign on the ‘GC Have Your Say’ platform.   This feedback was open from 22 August 
2024 and closed at 5pm on 19 September 2024. 
The responses were provided in the form of free text and have been reviewed to identify 
feedback themes to assist with summarisation. Within each theme, recurring comments 
have been noted to provide additional context. 
Listed below are the five major themes identified through the analysis:

Officer Comments 
The Council levies general rates on the property based on attributes of the property and 
how it is used. It is not possible to differentiate properties based on the circumstances of 
the property owner via differential general rates. 
Instead, Council will provide specific support to property owners, where appropriate, to 
assist in paying the rates and charges for their property. Support measures include: 
• 
Pensioner rate rebates to reduce the rates liability.   
• 
Financial Hardship support. 
• 
Interest free payment plans that allows their rates and charges to be paid off over 
time. 
To access any of these support measures, ratepayers should contact the Council’s 
Customer contact team.

Officer Comments 
The decision to change the rating structure for PPR vertical CTS is not related to ‘taxing a 
view’ and the colloquial term ‘view tax’ given to the change is misleading. 
The 2024-25 rating structure was implemented to assist in addressing an inequity with the 
general rates levied on vertical CTS units that exists because of the way that rateable 
valuations for CTS lots are determined. The new structure recognises factors that affect 
the value of a unit, namely the size (floor area) and floor level above ground and applies 
an increase to the rate and minimum general rate as these attributes increase. 
A fundamental principle of general rates is that higher value properties ought to pay more 
general rates than lower valued properties used in the same way. The method in which 
rateable valuations must be calculated for vertical CTS properties does not, on its own, 
provide a reliable and consistent method of ensuring the principle of valuation-based 
rating is adhered to.

Officer Comments 
Body corporate fees pay for costs associated with maintenance and insurance of 
buildings, facilities, internal roads, or structures within the body corporate land. These 
costs are not unique to body corporates and property owners outside of a CTS also incur 
similar cost for the maintenance and upkeep of their properties. The amount of body 
corporate fees is irrelevant for the purpose of calculating general rates.  
Similarly, the purchase price paid by a ratepayer for their unit represents an investment in 
an asset by the property owner (to purchase the property) and does not negate the fact 
that the property must contribute to the operations of Council through general rates.

General rates are calculated by applying the rate in the dollar for the rating category 
assigned to a specific property to the rateable valuation for the property. This method of 
calculation, which is mandated in the Local Government Act 2009, implies that higher 
valued properties pay higher general rates that lower valued properties used in the same 
way. 
The increase in general rates for PPR vertical CTS properties brought about by the rating 
structure change in 2024-25, is not implying they use more services or facilities than 
previously – but merely an increase to ensure an appropriate contribution is being made 
through general rates in accordance with the principles of valuation-based rating.

The change to the differential general rating structure was considered by the Council 
during confidential budget meetings in April and May; however changes made to rating 
policy through the budget process are not able to be communicated until the budget is 
adopted. For the 2024-25 financial year, the budget was adopted on Friday 7 June 2024. 
Given the relatively short period of time between budget adoption and the issue of the

communication with the rates notice instead of an additional separate correspondence

Improvements to communication of changes are being considered.

Background
•
The City adopted new rating category structure for general rate on PPR Vertical Community Titles Scheme 
(CTS) lots commencing 1 July 2024
•
Same rating structure that has been applied to Non-Principal Place of Residence / Permanent Rentals 
(NPPR/PR) and Tourist Rental (TR) vertical CTS lots since 2006
•
In response to the changes, 20 petitions have been received from owners of affected CTS lots and 548 
responses have been received during a Community Feedback campaign.

The 2024-25 Adopted Rating Structure

The change is about ensuring that all properties make a meaningful and fair contribution through 
general rates that is consistent with the principles of valuation-based rating.  Higher valued 
properties pay more general rates than lower valued properties used in the same way.

No 'extra’ revenue was raised from the change. The change resulted in a redistribution of revenue 
across the rate base.  

If these changes had not been made to PPR Vertical CTS the remaining 96% of properties across 
the City would have experienced higher general rate increases.

The change was to improve equity and fairness for all ratepayers across the City.

The Valuer General does not provide individual valuations for each lot within a CTS, instead, one 
valuation is issued for the CTS property that is ‘shared’ between the lots in the CTS.  

Approximately 12,200 properties, or 4% of all ratepayers across the City, were affected by the 
change.  

The increases in dollar terms are between $3.20 per week for those on levels 5 to 10, up to a 
maximum of $13 per week for properties located above level 40.  97 properties across the City 
received the maximum increase of approximately $13 per week (less than 1% of affected 
properties and less than 0.03% of all rateable properties).

The 2024-25 Adopted Rating Structure

In the 2024-25 financial year, total rates and charges for a PPR single unit dwelling with a median 
rateable valuation increased by just 4.24%, which is less than the average inflation for the 
previous 12 months of 4.8%.  

Whilst the general rate for the vertical PPR CTS in 2024-25 has increased, this is to achieve a 
more equitable general rate than was previously levied. 

Approximately 51% of residential properties across the City are part of a CTS. The number of 
vertical CTS properties will also continue to grow in line with increased density. 

The City landscape has changed, therefore if this was not corrected it would further exacerbate 
the issue.

The City’s rating structure needs to ensure appropriate and equitable general rates are levied on 
all ratepayers and to make sure that non-CTS properties are not levied a disproportionately high 
contribution towards General Rates. 

Aligns with the existing structure in place for Non-Principal Place of Residence / Permanent 
Rentals and Tourist Rental vertical CTS lots that has been in place since 2006.

The changes have been extensively tested with reference to market valuations and is consistent 
with the principle of valuation based rating.

What do other Councils do?
Brisbane City – have developed a ‘parity factor’ that applies to large vertical CTS 
based on the site valuation assigned to the land on which the CTS complex is built.  
City of Moreton Bay – have adopted 12 differential general rating categories (6 for 
PPR and 6 for NPPR) that are banded based on a calculation of the area per square 
metre of the land on which the CTS is constructed. 
Sunshine Coast – have adopted 6 differential general rating categories (2 each for 
PPR, NPPR and TR).  For each property type there is one category applicable to 
Low-Rise units for CTS complexes of no more than 4 levels, and one for High-Rise
units for CTS complexes greater than 4 levels.
