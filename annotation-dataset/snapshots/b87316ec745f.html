Iranian-Linked Media Falsely Claim Australia Has “Only 18 Days” of Fuel Reserves, Overstating the Impact of the War
By Waqar Rizvi
What’s happening: There is no shortage of evidence that the war in Iran and the virtual closure of the pivotal Strait of Hormuz has led to oil and gas shocks around the globe. Nevertheless, Iran is overstating the impact of the war on energy flows, in an apparent effort to sow additional discord among U.S. allies over the U.S.-Israel attack on the Islamic Republic.
Iranian state-aligned media outlets now falsely claim that Australia has fewer than 18 days of fuel reserves remaining, which is roughly half the size of the reserves actually reported by the Australian government and other sources. The claim is being advanced with the help of a Google AI editing tool.
Context: Since the Feb. 28, 2026, U.S.-Israel attack, Iran has effectively blocked the Strait of Hormuz, a narrow waterway between Iran and Oman through which roughly 20 percent of the world’s oil supply passes. The International Energy Agency said in a March 12 report that the Middle East conflict was “creating the largest supply disruption in the history of the global oil market.”
A closer look: With the oil disruption emerging as perhaps Iran’s best weapon in the three-week-old conflict, Iran state media has been hyping, and in some cases, over-hyping, the impact of the Strait of Hormuz’s closure. On March 13, Tasnim News, which is linked to Iran’s Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps, published an article stating in Farsi: “Australia’s energy minister has announced: we only have 18 days of petrol, 16 days of diesel and 14 days of jet fuel.”
- The article was accompanied by a graphic that included the above-cited data, which was attributed to Australia’s Energy Minister Chris Bowen and to Australia’s Department of Climate Change, Energy, Environment and Water. The claim and graphic quickly spread among other pro-Iran outlets and social media accounts, garnering tens of thousands of views.
Actually: The figures cited by the Iran-linked sources are false, representing only half of Australia’s actual reserves. The graphic was digitally manipulated, likely using a Google AI tool, NewsGuard found.
- In a March 11 speech to Parliament about the impact of the war in the Middle East, Australian Energy Minister Chris Bowen said: “I can guarantee that Australia enters this crisis much better prepared.… We enter this crisis with 36 days of petrol.” He added that Australia had “36 days of petrol supply on hand, 29 days of jet fuel and 32 days of diesel.”
- A graphic showing these figures was published on March 14 by the Australian public broadcaster Special Broadcasting Service (SBS News).
The version of the graphic advanced by Iran was digitally altered. The manipulated version preserves the overall design and structure of the original Australian broadcaster’s graphic, but replaces the figures with values reduced by half. It also includes the Google Gemini AI logo in the bottom-right corner, suggesting the numbers were likely altered using a Google AI editing tool. Google’s press office did not respond to an emailed request for comment.
The false claim about Australia’s fuel reserves appears to be part of a wider Iranian narrative, exaggerating the impact of the Strait of Hormuz crisis.
- On March 11, Iranian state broadcaster Press TV published an article stating that the closure of Hormuz “could trigger a full‑blown energy catastrophe for the West” and warning that Western and U.S.‑aligned economies would face “crippling fuel shortages.”
