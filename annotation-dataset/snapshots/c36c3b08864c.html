Sorry, the viral video of drones creating a skyscraper-sized skeleton over Dubai isn’t real
CLAIM: Drones were used to create a giant, illuminated skeleton near the Burj Khalifa for a Halloween display above pedestrians in an outdoor plaza.
AP’S ASSESSMENT: False. The video was fabricated. Experts said there are tell-tale signs that the “drone show” is not real, including that it moves faster than drone technology currently allows. In addition, the crowd below the skeleton shows no reaction to the mammoth display above them.
THE FACTS: Since Halloween, the video spread widely on social media with claims that it showed a nighttime display that reached nearly as high as the Burj Khalifa, the tallest building in the world.
In the 8-second clip, the skeleton — made up of what seem to be illuminated drones — takes a giant step forward while floating directly above pedestrians milling around an outdoor plaza next to Dubai Mall, one of the largest shopping centers in the world. The skeleton nearly matches the skyscraper’s height of 2,716 feet and 6 inches (828 meters).
“Dubai’s #Halloween drone show takes an eerie turn with a spooky skeleton in the sky,” reads one TikTok post that shared the video. It had been viewed approximately 8.5 million times as of Friday.
Other users put a political spin on the frightful presentation, including a post on X, formerly Twitter, which states: “The UAE holds an incredible drone simulation of a giant skeleton in Burj Khalifa, Dubai on Halloween. The decision sparked outrage among many muslim countries, who view Halloween as a ‘satanic holiday,’ celebrated during the terrible events in Gaza.”
But multiple experts told The Associated Press that the video does not really show a skeleton built out of drones.
“That’s 100% fake,” said Preston Ward, chief pilot and general counsel for Sky Elements Drone Shows in Fort Worth, Texas, who also debunked the display on social media.
Ward pointed to a number of clues in the video that indicate the skeleton is not what it seems. For example, it moves too fast for what drone technology currently allows, there is not enough noise coming from what he estimates to be about 2,000 drones, and there is an unlikely flawlessness to the display.
“It looks too perfect, too,” he said. “You’re always going to have some wiggle. You’re always going to have something in a live show. And all their colors look the same, the glows look the same.”
Additionally, flying drones over pedestrians in a crowded area like the one in the video is not safe, according to Ryan Sigmon, co-founder of Firefly Drone Shows in Detroit, who agreed that the skeleton does not look real.
Beyond technological and logistical concerns, no one in the video is reacting to the behemoth above them and this is the only video from the alleged show that has appeared online — improbable in an age when most people carry a smartphone. No such show was announced or advertised in Dubai either.
While it’s unclear who made the video, it was posted on TikTok on Tuesday by Geoscan Group, a company that produces drone shows, where it received millions of views. Geoscan did not respond to a request for comment about the video, and the clip was no longer on its TikTok account as of Friday.
