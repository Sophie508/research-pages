Russian IL-20 Electronic Intelligence aircraft conducted two surveillance flights around Japan in the space of a week, Japan’s Joint Staff Office said Thursday.
A Russian IL-20 flew in Wednesday from the Russian continent over the Sea of Okhotsk and into the Pacific Ocean, according to a Joint Staff Office (JSO) news release. The IL-20 subsequently flew south until it was off the coast of the Iwate Prefecture, located on the main island of Honshu, before the aircraft turned around. The surveillance aircraft then flew over the Sea of Okhotsk and into the Sea of Japan.
A map included with the release shows the Russian aircraft flying over the southern tip of Russia’s Sakhalin Island and onward to the Catherine Strait, which separates Kunashir Island and Iturup Island, two of the southern Kuril Islands held by Russia but claimed by Japan. In response, fighter aircraft from the Japan Air Self-Defense Force’s (JASDF) Northern Air Defense Command were scrambled.
Wednesday’s observed flight followed a similar flight conducted Saturday. A Russian IL-20 ELINT aircraft flew in from the Russian continent, traveling southwest over the Sea of Japan before changing course off the coast of Kyogamisaki, Kyoto Prefecture. The aircraft then headed northwest toward the Russian continent. Fighter aircraft from the JASDF’s Northern Air Defense Command and other units were also scrambled to respond to the Russian aircraft.
Sovereign airspace is restricted to 12 nautical miles from a country’s baseline, but a number of countries―including Japan―maintain an Air Defense Identification Zone that extends beyond 12 nautical miles to identify approaching aircraft in international airspace. These ADIZs are not recognized under international law.
Russian surveillance aircraft routinely operate off Japan in international airspace, but it is unclear how frequent the flights are because the JSO only releases reports of those the Japan Defense Ministry deems of public interest.
New Japan Defense Minister Shinjiro Koizumi recently indicated Russian flights are occurring near Japan on a daily basis.
“Russia is conducting active military operations on a daily basis around our country even as it carries out the invasion of Ukraine—this is the reality,” Koizumi wrote in the Oct. 24 social media post, which was translated from Japanese.
Koizumi’s statement coincided with two Russian Tu-95 bombers and two Russian Su-35 fighter jets (Su-35) flying near Japan. The four aircraft approached from the Russian mainland, traveling southeast over the Sea of Japan and turned northeast off the coast of Sado Island.
The aircraft flew over international waters, but the flight path was close to Japanese airspace, according to Japan’s JSO, which released details on the observed flight Oct. 24. Fighter aircraft from the Japan Air Self-Defense Force’s (JASDF) Central Air Defense Command were scrambled in response.
The Russian Ministry of Defense, on the same day, issued a release stating that Tu-95MS strategic missile carriers from Russia’s long-range aviation force carried out a scheduled flight in the airspace over the neutral waters of the Sea of Japan. The flight duration was more than 11 hours. Fighter escort was provided by Su-35S and Su-30SM aircraft of the Russian Aerospace Forces and at certain stages of the route, the bombers were escorted by fighter jets from foreign countries.
“Long-range aviation crews regularly fly over the neutral waters of the Arctic, North Atlantic, Pacific Ocean, and the Black and Baltic seas… All flights by Russian Aerospace Forces aircraft are carried out in strict compliance with international airspace regulations,” reads the Russian Defense Ministry news release.
Japan also sent aircraft to monitor two People’s Liberation Army Navy (PLAN) frigates this week, which made separate transits in the waters between Okinawa and Miyako Island to enter the Philippine Sea.
The Japan Maritime Self-Defense Force (JMSDF) observed PLAN frigate CNS Binzhou (515) on Monday as it sailed south around 50 miles west of Kume Island and onward between Okinawa and Miyako Island to enter the Philippine Sea. Later the same day, PLAN frigate CNS Anyang (599) was spotted by Japanese forces as it made the same journey to enter the Philippine Sea.
JMSDF P-3C Maritime Patrol Aircraft (MPA) shadowed the PLAN ships.
“China has been intensifying its military activities around our country, frequently continuing to advance into the Pacific beyond the so-called first island chain, as seen in this instance,” Koizumi shared Tuesday on social media platform X. “As the Minister of Defense, I will stand at the forefront of the personnel maintaining 24-hour surveillance and ensure the utmost readiness for our nation’s defense.”
