SAN DIEGO — The San Diego-based aircraft carrier USS Abraham Lincoln played a significant role in Saturday morning’s coordinated operation against Iran.
The USS Abraham Lincoln, which is homeported at Naval Air Station North Island, has been positioned in the Arabian Sea for weeks.
It's a nuclear-powered vessel known for its role in various operations, including support for Operation Enduring Freedom in Afghanistan and Operation Southern Watch.
The ship is providing a mobile airfield for U.S. Navy and Marine Corps aircraft supporting the mission in Iran.
"It's got about 70-75 aircraft and different types on there. It's a floating city. It even has its own television station. You've got thousands of enlisted sailors on there," Armen Kurdian, a retired U.S. Navy Captain Pilot, said.
Cpt. Kurdian once called the USS Abraham Lincoln home for six months.
"It's got two nuclear reactors at the same time. You can launch aircraft. It's got four different catapults," Kurdian said. "The way this ship can launch and recover aircraft, it is a well-oiled machine."
According to a post from the U.S. Central Command's (CENTCOM) X account, Iran's Revolutionary Guard Corps claims to have struck the U.S.S. Abraham Lincoln with ballistic missiles. But CENTCOM reports that it is a "lie."
"The Lincoln was not hit. The missiles launched didn’t even come close. The Lincoln continues to launch aircraft in support of CENTCOM’s relentless campaign to defend the American people by eliminating threats from the Iranian regime," CENTCOM said.
Meanwhile, Coronado Police report that until further notice, expect traffic delays around all Naval Base Coronado entrance gates due to increased base security measures.
