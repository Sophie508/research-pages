Multilingual Claims

Non-English fact-checks through our pipeline: 10 claims, 59 evidence statements extracted, 42 kept. Claim languages: Arabic 2, Bengali 1, French 1, German 1, Hindi 2, Portuguese 1, Spanish 1, Telugu 1. All are text claims from the VeriTaS claim browser whose fact-checking article cites outside sources; seven were picked because the event is local and the sources are in the local language. The extractor reads the original-language article and writes each evidence statement in English; the statement is also given in the article's language, and the quoted passage it came from is kept as written. Links come from the pipeline's URL stage, the procedure the annotation interface uses: the article's own hyperlinks first (ranked against the quoted passage), then a web search restricted to pages dated before the claim. An LLM reads each linked page and checks that it states the evidence; when it does not, the statement is cut back to what the page says, or other pages are tried, and a statement no page supports is dropped. The verdict comes from the pipeline's merge and verdict prompts with the English claim. The cards show the run whose web search used the English statement; the section below the cards compares it with a run whose search used the statement in the article's language. "Highlighted" opens the page with the supporting passages marked, or a text copy of it. The language of each linked page is read from a marker in its address, otherwise detected from the page text at 90% confidence or more. Linked-page languages: English 25, unknown 7, Portuguese 5, Hindi 3, uncertain 1, German 1. Ten claims are too few for rates; this page is for checking quality.

English search vs search in the article's language

The same ten claims, run twice. The two runs differ only in the language of the web search used when the article's own links do not support a statement: the English statement, or the statement in the article's language. Everything else is the same: the extraction, the article's links (ranked against the quoted passage), the check of every page, the merge and verdict prompts with the English claim. These claims have no gold evidence, so there is no EV2R score; the VeriTaS verdict is given for reference.

Counts. Across the 10 claims: the verdict differs between the two runs for 1; the attached links differ for 6, with 4 links found only through the original-language search and 7 only through the English one. Evidence kept: 42 (English search) and 43 (original-language search), of which 9 and 11 link to a page in the claim's language, and 34 and 30 carry a highlight.

Verdicts. Nine of the ten claims get the same verdict from both runs. The German claim (padlocks on motorcycle brakes) gets Refuted from one run and Conflicting / cherry-picking from the other, but the verdict prompt received exactly the same input in both runs: asked three more times with that input, the verdict model answered Refuted twice and Conflicting once for one run, and once and twice for the other. The difference is the verdict model's own variation on a borderline claim, not the search. The Spanish and French claims come out Conflicting / cherry-picking in both runs where VeriTaS says false: both claims mix a true part (the diesel is low-sulphur and comes from Deer Park; health agencies do not recommend organised screening) with a false one, and the verdict prompt reads that as misleading rather than false.

Where the search language mattered. Lebanon: with the English search none of the five statements kept a supporting page; searching in Arabic found the Lebanese National News Agency report, and two statements were kept with it. France: the French search added a page of the French National Cancer Institute; the English search added JAMA, the US Preventive Services Task Force and a Wiley journal instead. The English search also added a Times of India report (Hindi recharge scam) and an OPB article (Telugu claim, a press conference in Washington).

Where it did not. For four claims (Hindi firecrackers, Portuguese, Bengali, German) the two runs attach exactly the same links: every statement got its link from the fact-check article itself, so the web search was never used.

Limits. Ten claims, mostly chosen for local events; the counts above describe these claims only. A claim whose statements are all dropped still gets a verdict, because the verdict prompt also reads the justification written at extraction. Page languages come from a detector that sometimes fails on the same page, so the per-run counts of local-language pages can differ by one for that reason alone.
