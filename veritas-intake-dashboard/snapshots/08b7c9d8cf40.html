TAMPA, Fla. (WJW) – “It’s time to ban pajamas…”
Tampa International Airport is making a statement about what people wear when they fly.
“Pajamas. At the airport. In the middle of the day,” Tampa International Airport wrote in a social media post.
Of course, this follows the transportation secretary’s call to “dress with respect” when traveling.
Before holiday travel at the end of last year, Transportation Secretary Sean Duffy issued a plea.
“I call this, just maybe dressing with some respect. You know, whether it’s a pair of jeans and a decent shirt. I would encourage people to maybe dress a little bit better, which encourages, encourages us to maybe behave a little better. Let’s try not to wear slippers and pajamas as we come to the airport,” he said in November 2025.
Tampa International is onboard with the moves.
In a cheeky social media post, they claimed to have already banned Crocs and have given “everyone the amazing opportunity to experience the world’s first Crocs-free airport…”
“We’ve seen enough, we’ve had enough,” the post said about pajamas.
“The madness stops today,” they said.
“Help Tampa International Airport become the first Crocs-free and pajama-free airport.”
“Do your part. Say no to pajamas…”
Of course, travelers looking for comfort may have a different opinion on the matter.
Not to mention the likelihood of lengthy delays and cancellations.
According to FlightAware, there were nearly 1,800 delays in the U.S. before noon on Thursday.
Commenters on the post added some reasons they’re traveling in comfort.
“I realize that in the days of yore, people dressed up for air travel. This was also the first cabin then. People only care about price. The PJs stay,” one wrote.
“I can’t support this, having sat at the terminal from 10 PM until nearly 4 AM on a flight delay,” wrote another.
Another said, “I think a lot of people have also had enough. Of airports and airlines. We’re treated like cattle with rare exceptions, stuffed into tiny little seats. … We pay exorbitant prices for food and drink at the airport and then receive peanuts and a biscuit for a meal on the plane.”
FOX 8 sister station WFLA got a statement from Tampa International Airport.
“Tampa International Airport regularly shares lighthearted, satirical social media content as part of our ongoing effort to engage with our followers. Today’s post about ‘banning’ pajamas was another playful nod to day-of-travel fashion debates. We encourage our passengers to travel comfortably and appreciate our loyal followers who enjoy the online humor.”
