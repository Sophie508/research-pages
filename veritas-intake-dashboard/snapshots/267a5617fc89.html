Black sesame seeds: Nutritional value, health benefits, and food industrial applications
Graphical abstract
Introduction
Sesame (Sesamum indicum L.), a member of the Pedaliaceae family, ranks ninth among the edible oilseed crops worldwide for high oil content and economic potential (Panero et al., 2022). Sesame production is widespread throughout the world, primarily in Asia and African countries such as China, India, Pakistan, Tanzania, and Sudan, which share about 96% of the world's production (Kim et al., 2020). There are many different varieties of sesame, displaying contrasting genetic dissimilarities in morpho-economic traits and quality attributes, such as seed shape, length, and color, varying from white, yellow, and brown, to black (Wang et al., 2020). Among them, black sesame seeds (BSS) have long been considered a superior health food to other ones in Asian countries and are recognized as a traditional medicinal food and a condiment in many cultures around the world.
As long ago as the Ming Dynasty of China, the classic “Compendium Materia Medica” compiled by Li ShiZhen, has recorded that “extracting oil from sesame seeds to prioritize the use of white ones, and considering black ones suitable for consumption”. BSS has been listed in the Chinese Pharmacopoeia as a traditional Chinese medicine with liver- and kidney-nourishing properties (Li, Li, et al., 2020). Additionally, BSS is beneficial to human health for its antioxidant, anti-inflammatory, anticancer, neuroprotective, antihypertensive, and antidiabetic properties (Dutta et al., 2022; Saisum et al., 2020). The edible values and medicinal functions of BSS are highly connected to its abundance of nutrients composed of proteins, carbohydrates, lipids, minerals, lignans, black pigment, etc (Ahmed et al., 2021). Over the last 15–20 years, there has been growing interest in developing functional foods that offer additional health benefits beyond basic nutrition, with BSS being an unparalleled ingredient in functional foods. Strengthening the research on the nutritional component and pharmacological activities of BSS is of profound significance for the development of relevant health foods. However, there is a lack of scientifically based information about their nutritional profiles, bioactive properties, and potential applications of BSS.
In this review, we aim to provide a comprehensive and systematic overview of the latest research progress in BSS from the perspectives of phytochemical composition and nutritive value, health benefits, and applications in the current food industry. The primary macro- and micro-nutrients of BSS are reviewed first, and the effect of processing and genotypes on the nutritional profile of BSS is discussed. A summary of recent advances in health benefits is included. Finally, the review depicts the utilization of BSS as an ingredient in various kinds of functional food products and food packaging materials. This review will contribute to a better understanding of BSS and shed new light on the development of innovative black sesame-based products.

Nutritional profile
The unique chemical composition of BSS endows its diverse biological importance. BSS mainly contains approximately 20% protein and 50% lipid, as well as significant amounts of minerals and black pigment. Applications in food products
The considerable nutritional value of BSS has garnered attention from both food manufacturers and researchers for incorporating them into a range of food systems. Thus far, successful integration has been achieved in tahini, bakery products, composite beverages, yoghurt products, and even food packaging (Fig. 4A and Table 3). Conclusions and future perspectives
This review initially provides an overview of the primary chemical components and nutritional values of BSS, followed by an examination of their potential health advantages, thereby establishing their application within the food industry. However, there are currently insufficient studies on its chemical components, such as the structures and content, the appropriate separation and purification method, and identification methods, especially for lignans and black pigments.
