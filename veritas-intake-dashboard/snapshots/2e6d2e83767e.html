CBE responds to flak over Fairview School Ramadan accommodations for food-free space
Some commenters expressed discontent over changes to the lunch schedule and the use of the cafeteria as a food-free space
Calgary’s public school board is trying to clear the air over a social media post regarding how a southeast school is accommodating Muslim students observing Ramadan this month.
The social media post, from Sunday, included a screenshot of a Feb. 18 email that was sent to parents of children who attend Fairview School.
The post highlighted that to support Muslim students who may be fasting during the day, the school’s cafeteria would be a designated “no food space” for the first half of the lunch hour for students in grades 4 to 6. The lunch room would be a designated “no food space” for students in grades 7 through 9 for the entire lunch hour.
On inclement weather days, the school’s learning commons would be a “no food space” for all students in grades 4 to 9, according to the email.
Ramadan, a holy month in Islam, began Feb. 17 and is set to end on Thursday. The month sees Muslims fast from sunrise to sunset.
“Ramadan is a month of reflection, prayer, generosity and community,” the email from Fairview School states. “We extend our warm wishes to all who are observing, and remain committed to ensuring our school is a supportive and respectful environment.”
Some commenters on the post expressed discontent that the school was altering lunchtime operations and asked why other areas of the school, other than the cafeteria, could not be designated as no-food spaces.
In its own social media post over the weekend, the Calgary Board of Education addressed the situation, though did not mention Fairview School.
“The school in the social media post always has different grades eating in different spaces in the building,” the post said, adding that students are still eating in designated lunch spaces.
“During special cultural and religious observances, schools are able to make accommodations to support students, both in and out of classroom activities. This could include accommodations when students are fasting during Ramadan,” the post said.
CBE’s post added that depending on the number of students and size of the school population engaging in fasting, accommodation can be done in different ways, including designating distinct spaces as non-food areas.
“During Ramadan, alternative foodless spaces are available for fasting students so they are not around others who are eating.”
In a statement to Postmedia on Monday, a CBE spokesperson added that the school board has a duty to accommodate students practicing their religion in schools, with an approach guided by Administrative Regulation 3067 – Religion in Education.
“The definition of reasonable accommodation can differ based on various factors, such as the specific needs of a student, the available resources and facilities of the school, and potential impact on others,” the statement said.
“If CBE can accommodate as requested, we should do so.”
