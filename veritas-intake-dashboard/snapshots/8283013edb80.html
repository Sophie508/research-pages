Did the Tampa International Airport Really Just Ban Pajamas? The Florida transit hub has posted a dress code mandate on their social media accounts that touts a ban on sleepwear as well as Crocs By Raven Brunner Published on February 26, 2026 02:09PM EST 31 Comments Tampa International Airport. Credit : Paul Hennessy/Anadolu Agency via Getty NEED TO KNOW The Tampa International Airport announced a new ban on pajamas in a viral social media post on Feb. 26 “Help Tampa International Airport become the world’s first Crocs-free and pajama-free airport," the airport wrote in an X post that has garnered over 1.8 million views Tampa International Airport said in a statement that the post was a joke and encouraged passengers "to travel comfortably" Did an airport in Florida just ban pajamas? Many people were left in shock when the Tampa International Airport announced an alleged ban on pajamas on Thursday, Feb. 26. “After successfully banning Crocs and giving everyone the amazing opportunity to experience the world’s first Crocs-free airport, it’s time to take on an even larger crisis,” the airport wrote in a viral X post that has garnered over 1.8 million views. The crisis, the airport said, is people wearing pajamas at the airport “in the middle of the day.” Stock image of a woman wearing loungewear in an airport. Getty “We know this decision could be disruptive to someone in your life. It’s time to have a difficult conversation with them. You can do this,” Tampa Airport continued in the post. “The madness stops today. The movement starts now,” the airport wrote. “Help Tampa International Airport become the world’s first Crocs-free and pajama-free airport.” Tampa International Airport then urged visitors to “do your part” and “say no to pajamas." Our new app is here! Free, fun and full of exclusives. Scan to download now! X users had a mixed response to the social media post, with some asking for details on how the alleged ban would be implemented. One commenter asked if sweat pants would be “considered pajamas.” Tampa International Airport said that the post was a joke in a statement obtained by PEOPLE. "Tampa International Airport regularly shares lighthearted, satirical social media content as part of our ongoing effort to engage with our followers," the statement reads. "Today’s post about 'banning' pajamas was another playful nod to day-of-travel fashion debates. We encourage our passengers to travel comfortably and appreciate our loyal followers who enjoy the online humor," the airport continued. The airport's account has a history of trolling its followers with online gags. Tampa International Airport. Mike Ehrmann/Getty Tampa Airport’s social media manager C.J. Johnson delivered a speech at the University of South Florida in September 2025, where he spoke about his approach to the brand’s online voice. The aforementioned Crocs ban, for instance, was a joke that went viral. “You have to give people a lot of sweets, so when you need to feed them a vegetable they’ll listen,” Johnson said in his presentation. “If I got on every day at Tampa International Airport and tweeted we have flights today, who would care? No one.” “If we do funny silly stuff like this with targeted messages that grow your audience and then I say, ‘Hey guys, by the way, we are closing the airport due to a hurricane,’ then people lean in and listen,” he claimed. The social media post comes amid Secretary of Transportation Sean Duffy repeatedly insisting that people should dress up while traveling. "I can't mandate you dress up, but don't wear pajamas and slippers to the airport. I think if you dress a little better, you behave a little better," he said on NewsNation's Katie Pavlich Tonight in January. "We're all getting into pretty tight airline seats. The prices go down when we do that. But I want people to be well behaved and I think a little better dressed would help with that," the administrator added. Duffy expressed a similar sentiment last November while speaking about the department's new “Golden Age of Travel” campaign at Newark Airport. "I would encourage people to dress a little better, which encourages us to maybe behave a little better," he said.
