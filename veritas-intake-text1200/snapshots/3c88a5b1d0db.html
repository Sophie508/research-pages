Introduction and background
This audit forms part of the 2007/2008 Internal Audit Plan, which has been approved by the
Mayor and Audit Panel. The plan entails evaluation of the control environment established
and applied to the E-Counting system.
The arrangements for the 2008 London Elections for Mayor, London and Constituency
Assembly Member includes a solution for the electronic counting (E-Counting) of votes and
the London Elects team, which was established to manage the delivery of this solution.
The primary objective of this audit is to provide an independent assurance evaluation on the
adequacy of the control framework established and applied to the achievement of the
electronic counting system. The audit assurance is based on an assessment on the level of
compliance achieved by the E-counting implementation arrangements with the following
best practice standards:
•
ISO 27001 - Information Security;
Management Of Risk (M_O_R), as advocated by Central Government; and
Information Technology Infrastructure Library Business Continuity requirements.
The work was based on a series of gap analysis reviews of current practice against the
appropriate standards being employed by both the Contractor and London Elects. This
resulted in a Phase 1 interim report being issued which also included an action plan for the
interim recommendations before the Phase 2 internal audit work was undertaken to verify
and update the status of previously agreed phase 1 actions and to finalise the audit report
and Gap Analysis.

2. Management of Risk
The Management of Risk (MoR) best practice guidance was used to identify any gaps in
the risk management framework (see Appendix B of this report). This included an analysis
of the risk strategy, principles, and approach to risk management and controls to assess
the extent to which risk is embedded in the E-Counting system.
One recommendation was raised to help minimise risks associated with business
continuity activities and this recommendation cross references to 27001 and ITIL business
continuity requirements. The recommendation is for testing of the Business Continuity
Plans.

6. ORGANISATION OF INFORMATION SECURITY
6.1 Internal organisation. To manage information security within the organisation.
6.1.1
commitment to
Management support for Information Security within the GLA has been demonstrated through the
establishment of an Information Security Programme and the assignment of information security
responsibilities. The policy statement on the Information Security framework includes a commitment to
use ISO 27001 as a benchmark against which to measure progress. The ISIG, chaired by the Director
of Corporate Services meets monthly and follows up on any open actions on the action plan. The ISIG
Project Manager is also a team member of the E-Counting project.
The contract for the E-counting system includes a requirement for compliance with ISO27001.
6.1.2
co-ordination
The ISIG is responsible for all implementations of security initiatives and controls, with each member
assigned to represent each of the cross functional areas within the Council. Teams have been allocated
with responsibility areas for Information Security. They may be required to review the relevant policies,
procedures and work practices and report to the ISIG. The minutes of the Security Project Board
meetings include ownership and deadlines for items on the action plan. There is also a proviso to invite
other staff to join the ISIG where appropriate.
6.1.3
Allocation of
responsibilities
The E-Counting contract identifies the contractor as being responsible for the security of the system and
for providing a level of security which complies with ISO27001:2005.

have confirmed readiness for go-live. It is on this basis that GLRO approval for the E-counting system
was given at a meeting of the Election Steering Group (ESG) on 10.04.08.

9. PHYSICAL AND ENVIRONMENTAL SECURITY
9.1 Secure areas. To prevent unauthorised access, damage, and interference to the organisation’s premises and information.
Comments/Observations
9.1.1
Physical security
perimeter
Security at the London Lab, where count equipment servers will be stored before the count include
CCTV, windows alarmed and a 24/7 security presence.
In addition to the security provided by the count venues, contracts with security companies for the
provision of physical security at all 3 Count Centres are being negotiated. There are also ongoing
discussions with the Metropolitan Police for a heavy police presence at Count Centres and City Hall.
Count Centre’s layout plans have been prepared which show the location of scanners, PCs, servers
and the internal security.
9.1.2
Physical entry
controls
Contractor personnel will be required to produce a letter of appointment, a photo id and an id badge that
will be scanned to confirm the holder is on the database. Physical access restrictions to be imposed on
staff, agents, observers, press and visitors include bag searches before entry is permitted and colour
coded wrist bands to indicate areas where access is allowed.
9.1.3
Securing offices,
rooms and facilities
We were informed that the servers and telecommunications equipment will be in locked racking.
There will also be specific security cover for the server area for the entire period that the premises will
be occupied.
The lack of an effective, restrictive barrier separating the scanners, PCs and servers from agents and
observers is to be mitigated by the presence of security, contractor and borough personnel and police.
This arrangement is to balance security and transparency, as the whole process needs to be
witnessed by observers.
9.1.4
Protecting against
external and
environmental
threats
An independent Health and Safety assessment of the count centres is being carried out by an external
contractor, to be signed off by Facilities Management, and a report is due out imminently. Thus the
assessment of the effectiveness of fire detection, fire fighting, and air-con equipment and that flood
threats (from water pipes) are minimised has not been completed.
9.1.5
Working in secure
areas
The location of the Count Centres and London Lab is common knowledge; hence plans have been
made for a security inspection before they are occupied, and for a sustained security presence to be
maintained until the count is concluded. The Metropolitan Police have undertaken (verbally) to carry
out a security inspection of the Count Centres on the night of 1 May / morning of 2 May 2008, as close
as possible to the start of the count. The contracted security companies will begin their work on 30
April 2008 and will provide access control to the count areas from that date e.g. for IT equipment,
ballot boxes etc.

9.2 Equipment security. To prevent loss, damage, theft or compromise of assets and interruption of the organisations activities.
9.2.1

Site layout plans have been prepared. The server zone will be in a separate area. Physical access

We were informed that power and communication cables at the London Lab and Count Centres are

Maintenance and repair of equipment installed for E-Counting will be restricted to Contractor personnel,

Security of

Secure disposal or

Formal procedures for permanent deletion of the E-Counting system from the Contractor’s computers

9.2.7
Removal of property
All equipment will be controlled by the Contractor and may not leave the premises without authorisation

Audit Objectives
The audit was designed to ensure that management has established and applied an adequate and effective control environment for
the E-Counting system.
Audit Approach and Methodology
The audit approach was developed by an assessment of risks and management controls operating within each area of the scope.

• identification of the role and objectives of each area;
• identification of risks within the systems, and controls in existence to allow the control objectives to be achieved; and
