The European Central Bank (ECB) has greenlit the next phase of its digital euro initiative, paving the way for technical preparations ahead of a potential pilot program as early as 2027.
ECB President Christine Lagarde emphasized the project’s role in future-proofing Europe’s common currency, stating that efforts are underway to modernize both physical banknotes and introduce digital cash.
In a decision by the ECB’s Governing Council, the project moves into a preparation stage focused on ensuring readiness for the currency’s inaugural issuance. A final verdict on launching the digital euro – including an exact timeline – will only follow the adoption of supporting EU legislation. ECB estimates suggest that, if the legal framework is approved within the next year, a pilot could commence in 2027, with the Eurosystem potentially primed for a full debut by 2029.
Lagarde, addressing the broader vision, described the euro as “a trusted symbol of European unity.” She added: “We are working to make its most tangible form – euro banknotes and coins – fit for the future, to redesign and modernize our banknotes, and to prepare for the issuance of digital cash.”
Key Benefits for Europeans
The digital euro is designed to complement physical cash, extending its advantages – simplicity, privacy protection, reliability, and eurozone-wide availability – to digital transactions. It aims to:
– Safeguard user choice and privacy.
– Bolster Europe’s monetary sovereignty and economic security.
– Foster innovation in payments, making European systems more competitive, resilient, and inclusive.
The Eurosystem will proceed flexibly, aligning with calls from eurozone leaders for swift readiness while awaiting the completion of legislative processes. Concurrently, the ECB supports the European Commission‘s proposal to strengthen the legal right to pay with cash.
Timeline and Costs
– Pilot Phase: Possible start in 2027, contingent on legislation passage next year.
– Full Issuance: Eurosystem readiness targeted for 2029.
– Development Costs: Approximately €1.3 billion up to the first issuance, covering internal and external components.
– Annual Operating Costs: Around €320 million from 2029 onward, borne by the Eurosystem as a public good – similar to physical euro banknotes. These expenses are expected to be offset by seigniorage revenue, even with modest digital euro holdings compared to circulating notes.
For Greek citizens and businesses, the digital euro could enhance cross-border payments within the eurozone, reduce reliance on foreign digital platforms, and ensure continued access to a public, ECB-backed payment option amid rising cashless trends.
The ECB stresses that the initiative responds to evolving payment habits while preserving the euro’s core strengths. Further updates will depend on EU parliamentary progress.
