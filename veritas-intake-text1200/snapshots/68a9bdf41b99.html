Fishing in Spain: What do you need to know?
Updated: July 2026 · Reviewed by the Tom’s Catch team
Picture it: 7am on a Mediterranean quay, the diesel still cold, a skipper coiling lines while the sun climbs over the harbour wall. Spain is one of those rare countries where you can chase 200 kg bluefin tuna off Mallorca, big-game marlin off the Canaries, monster catfish on the Ebro and a lazy afternoon of sea bream off the Costa Brava — sometimes in the same week. But before you go, there are a few things worth getting right, and the fishing license rules changed in 2026.
The short version, if you’re in a hurry:
- License: a recreational sea-fishing license is issued by each autonomous community — not nationally. It’s free in most coastal regions but still mandatory. If you fish from a licensed charter, the boat covers it for you.
- New for 2026: the PescaREC app is now obligatory for declaring certain sea catches. It’s a catch-declaration duty, not a license — the charter’s captain handles compliance on board.
- Best all-rounders: Mallorca & the Balearics (tuna), the Canary Islands (marlin, big game), the Costa Brava & Valencia (Mediterranean inshore/offshore), and the Ebro (freshwater giants).
- Peak seasons: big tuna June–October; marlin and mahi-mahi late spring to autumn; inshore species fish well most of the year.
Whether you’re a first-timer on a family holiday or an angler ticking a species off your bucket list, here’s everything you need to know before you go.
Is Fishing Popular in Spain?
Absolutely. With more than 8,000 km of coastline across the Mediterranean, the Atlantic, the Bay of Biscay and two island archipelagos, plus some of Europe’s best inland rivers, Spain is a fishing nation through and through. Recreational sea fishing is hugely popular with both locals and visiting anglers, and the country hosts everything from quiet shore sessions to serious big-game offshore trips. The variety is the draw: cold Atlantic water in the north, warm blue Mediterranean in the east and south, and near-tropical volcanic drop-offs in the Canaries.
What Types of Fish Can Be Found in Spain?
Spain’s waters are genuinely diverse, and what you target depends heavily on where — and when — you go. Here’s a practical breakdown of the species anglers come for.
Saltwater species (the ones most anglers book for)
- Atlantic bluefin tuna — the headline act, especially around the Balearics and off Barcelona. Fish of 100–200 kg+ are landed every season.
- Blue & white marlin and mahi-mahi (dolphin fish) — big-game targets off the Canary Islands.
- Swordfish, amberjack (dentex/dentón), bonito, little tunny, mackerel — Mediterranean and Atlantic offshore.
- Sea bream, sea bass, grouper, snapper, mullet — reliable inshore species along the whole coast, great for beginners and families.
- Blue shark and other pelagics — deeper Atlantic and Canary waters.
Freshwater species
- Wels catfish — the Ebro River is one of Europe’s top spots, with fish over 90 kg (200 lb+).
- Carp, zander, pike and black bass — Ebro, plus the big reservoirs of Extremadura and Andalucía.
- Trout and Atlantic salmon — northern rivers in the Basque Country, Asturias and Cantabria.
Keep in mind: several species are protected or size/quota-limited, and catch-and-release is widely practised, particularly for big-game and freshwater specimens. Spanish rules on protected species are strict, and accidental catches of protected fish must be released immediately. Your skipper will know exactly what you can keep on the day.
Where to Fish in Spain
Spain rewards matching the destination to the fish you’re after. Here are the standout regions.
Mallorca & the Balearic Islands — bluefin tuna capital
Deep water close to shore makes the Balearics one of the Mediterranean’s premier tuna grounds. Boats out of Cala d’Or, Palma and Ibiza run for giant bluefin, with mahi-mahi and marlin also on the cards in the warmer months. This is bucket-list territory — see our tuna fishing guide for the full rundown.
The Canary Islands — big-game all year
Volcanic islands with 3,000 m of water almost within sight of the beach. Tenerife, Gran Canaria, Lanzarote and Fuerteventura are magnets for blue marlin, mahi-mahi, wahoo, tuna and shark. The near-tropical climate means something is biting every month of the year.
The Costa Brava & Barcelona — Mediterranean variety
Catalonia’s coast — Barcelona, Sitges, Palamós, the Ebro Delta and Tarragona — offers everything from relaxed inshore trips for bream and bass to serious offshore runs for bluefin. Easy to reach and ideal if you’re combining fishing with a city break.
Valencia & the Costa Blanca — warm-water inshore & offshore
Warm, productive Mediterranean water with a strong charter scene. Great for a mix of inshore species and offshore trolling, and a comfortable option for first-timers and families.
The Ebro River — freshwater giants
Spain’s most famous freshwater destination and one of Europe’s best for record-class catfish and specimen carp. Fishes year-round: carp in winter, catfish in summer, zander into autumn.
The green north — trout & salmon
The Basque Country, Cantabria and Asturias hold cold, clear rivers with wild trout and Atlantic salmon, plus a rugged Atlantic coast for bass and mackerel.
Fishing Techniques Commonly Used in Spain
- Trolling — dragging lures/baits for tuna, marlin and mahi-mahi offshore.
- Bottom & drift fishing — for bream, snapper, grouper and amberjack inshore.
- Jigging and spinning — increasingly popular for pelagics and bass.
- Fly fishing — northern trout and salmon rivers.
- Almadraba — the ancient trap-net bluefin tradition of the Andalusian coast (commercial/cultural, not something you’ll do on a charter, but part of Spain’s tuna heritage).
On a guided charter you don’t need to master any of these in advance — the crew rigs everything and shows you the ropes.
Is a Fishing License Required in Spain? (2026 Rules)
Yes — and this is the part most guides get wrong, because there is no single national fishing license in Spain. It’s devolved to each autonomous community, so the exact rules depend on the region and on whether you’re fishing salt water or fresh water.
Fishing from a charter? The easy answer
If you book a licensed charter, you almost never need your own license — the operator’s permit and the captain’s obligations cover the paying anglers on board. That’s one of the quiet advantages of going guided: no paperwork, no app to set up, no regional forms. You just show up and fish. (Always confirm at booking, but this is the norm.)
Fishing on your own? Here’s how the license works by region
The recreational sea-fishing license (licencia de pesca marítima de recreo) is issued by your autonomous community. The good news: it’s free in most coastal regions — including Galicia, Asturias, Cantabria, the Basque Country, Andalucía, Catalonia, the Valencian Community, the Balearic Islands, Murcia and the Canary Islands — but it’s still mandatory, and fishing without one is an infraction with fines from around €100 up to €3,000.
How to get it: apply online through the electronic office (sede electrónica) of the regional government where you’ll fish. For example, the Canary Islands and the Valencian Community both process it telematically, and Canarias even splits it by class (from-boat with trolling, spearfishing, or shore/boat without trolling).
For freshwater/inland fishing, licenses are also regional, but 10 regions share an inter-autonomous inland license that costs around €25 and lasts three years. You may also need a specific permit (coto) for certain rivers and reservoirs.
New for 2026: the PescaREC app
Since 20 March 2026 (Real Decreto 214/2026), Spain requires recreational sea anglers to declare catches of certain regulated species through the official, free PescaREC app from the Ministry of Agriculture, Fisheries and Food. Two things to remember:
- PescaREC is a catch-declaration duty, not a license. Having the app doesn’t let you fish; you still need your regional license.
- Declarable catches must be logged within 24 hours of finishing your session.
- On a charter, the captain handles this for you — one more reason going guided keeps it simple.
Rules and fees can change and vary by region — check your specific autonomous community before you fish independently.

When to go: quick season guide
- Bluefin tuna (Balearics, Barcelona): roughly June–October, peaking late summer.
- Marlin & mahi-mahi (Canaries): late spring through autumn; tuna April–September.
- Inshore (bream, bass, snapper): productive most of the year, best spring–autumn.
- Ebro catfish & carp: year-round; catfish best in the warm months.
All the Fishing: A Summary
Spain packs an extraordinary range of fishing into one country: giant tuna and marlin offshore, relaxed inshore days for the whole family, and record freshwater fish on the Ebro. Fishing in Spain: FAQ
Do I need a fishing license to fish in Spain?
Yes, but it depends how you fish. If you book a licensed charter, the operator’s license covers you on board — no paperwork needed. If you fish independently, you need a recreational sea-fishing license from the autonomous community where you’ll fish. It’s free in most coastal regions (Andalucía, Catalonia, Valencia, the Balearics, the Canaries, Galicia and more) but still mandatory.
What is the PescaREC app and do I need it in 2026?
PescaREC is the Spanish government’s free app, mandatory since 20 March 2026, for declaring catches of certain regulated sea species. It’s a catch-declaration duty, not a license, so it doesn’t replace your regional permit. On a charter, the captain handles the declaration for you.
What’s the best time of year to fish in Spain?
For giant bluefin tuna around the Balearics and Barcelona, June to October. For marlin and mahi-mahi in the Canary Islands, late spring through autumn. Inshore species like sea bream and bass fish well most of the year, and the Ebro’s catfish and carp bite year-round.
Where is the best fishing in Spain?
Mallorca and the Balearic Islands for bluefin tuna, the Canary Islands for big-game marlin, the Costa Brava and Valencia for varied Mediterranean fishing, and the Ebro River for record freshwater catfish and carp.
Can beginners go fishing in Spain?
Definitely. Many charters run family-friendly half-day inshore trips with all tackle provided and a skipper who guides you through everything. Just tell the captain your experience level when you book and they’ll match the trip to you.
