Paris 2024 taekwondo: All results, as Uzbekistan’s Ulugbek Rashitov defends Olympic title in men’s -68kg
After a thrilling night of taekwondo at the iconic Grand Palais, Uzbekistan’s Ulugbek Rashitov concluded the night for the men with a major win, defending his Olympic title in the men’s -68kg over Jordan’s Zaid Kareem, 2-0.
Kareem’s silver medal also carries a greater weight, as it marks the first time that Jordan’s flag will be flown in a medal ceremony thus far at the Olympic Games Paris 2024.
As the match kicked off, it was the Jordanian that landed the first kick to secure two points on the scoreboard, but Rashitov came back to take control of the first round, finishing 5-4.
The reigning champion’s confidence visibly grew as time counted down in the second round, taking an early lead for an uncontested 3-1 round two win, solidifying him as the back-to-back Olympic champion in the event.
Though Kareem had no choice but to settle for silver, he appeared thrilled to take second place to Rashitov, who he says “is my brother”.
“We always fight together. What we always say is that inside the court we are fighting, but outside the court we are brothers,” he said.
That fact was made obvious to the audience from the get-go, as the two had an arm around each other as they stood on the grand balcony to be introduced to the crowd, looking like they were about to do anything but physically fight for their spots on the podium.
So, one loss to a close friend after the three wins that brought him to the final was enough to please the Jordanian at his Olympic debut.
“What an amazing and tough day. I am so happy,” he said.
In the bronze medal matches that came just one hour before the main event, Brazil’s Edival Pontes defeated Spain’s Javier Perez Polo 2-1 to land on the podium, while People’s Republic of China’s Liang Yushuai secured his bronze after opponent Bradly Sinden of Great Britain withdrew due to a reported injury.
Taekwondo: Men’s -68kg podium
Gold: Ulugbek Rashitov, Uzbekistan
Silver: Zaid Kareem, Jordan
Bronze: Edival Pontes, Brazil
Bronze: Liang Yushuai, People’s Republic of China
